Umm privacy policy

Effective September 27, 2026

Umm is a voice keyboard and floating dictation button for Android made by Aadarsha Gopala Reddy. This policy explains what Umm does with your data. The short version: Umm has no server and no account. Your recordings go from your phone to OpenRouter, using your own OpenRouter key, and nowhere else.

What Umm records and sends

Umm records from the microphone only while its keyboard or floating button is actively listening. When you stop talking, Umm sends the recording to OpenRouter to be transcribed, then sends the transcript to OpenRouter again to be cleaned up (unless you choose the Raw level). OpenRouter passes each request to an AI model provider, such as OpenAI, Mistral, or Google, to do the work. Umm sends these requests with your OpenRouter key, so they fall under your agreement with OpenRouter and its privacy policy.

If a recording contains no speech, Umm discards it on the phone and sends nothing.

If you turn on zero data retention in your OpenRouter account's privacy settings, or choose "Zero data retention only" in Umm, Umm only uses models from providers that do not keep your data. To detect the account setting, Umm sends OpenRouter a few bytes of invalid audio; it contains nothing from your microphone.

Umm also asks OpenRouter for your key's name, spending, and credit limit to show them on the Account page, and for the list of available models.

What stays on your phone

  • Your OpenRouter key, encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore.
  • Your settings, app categories, and the names of apps you assigned to them.
  • The text of your last 50 dictations (History), and usage statistics such as word counts, durations, and cost.
  • A recording that failed to process, so you can retry it. It is deleted when the retry succeeds, or after 7 days.

None of this is sent to the developer. It is excluded from Android backups and device-to-device transfers, and it is deleted when you uninstall Umm. You can delete your history at any time in the app. In Settings → Stats you can hide the statistics on the home screen, stop recording them, or delete them.

What the keyboard can see

Like any keyboard, Umm is told which app a text field belongs to and what kind of field it is. Umm uses this to apply the cleanup level for that app and to turn itself off in password fields. Umm has no letter keys and does not read what you type with other keyboards. It only reads the character next to the cursor, to decide whether to add a space before or after the text it inserts.

What the floating button and Accessibility permission can see

If you turn on the optional floating button in Settings, Umm requests Android's Accessibility permission. Umm uses this permission solely to:

  • Detect when an editable text field is selected, so the button can appear and insert your dictated text.
  • Read the selected field's current text and cursor position, to correctly format spacing and insert text.
  • Read the foreground app's package name, to apply the cleanup level you chose for that app's category.

Umm never reads password fields (the floating button automatically hides and refuses recording over password fields) and never reads or stores anything else on your screen. No screen content is ever saved or sent off your phone. As with the keyboard, only your spoken audio goes to OpenRouter with your key.

What Umm does not do

  • No analytics, advertising, or tracking.
  • No Umm account, and no data sent to the developer.
  • No selling or sharing of your data.

Signing in with OpenRouter

"Connect with OpenRouter" opens OpenRouter's sign-in page in your browser. After you approve, OpenRouter returns a one-time code through a page on this website, which hands it back to the app. The code is useless without a secret that never leaves your phone. The app exchanges it for a new API key in your OpenRouter account, which you can see and delete in OpenRouter's key settings.

Children

Umm is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this address with a new effective date. The history of changes is public in the project repository.

Reporting output

The History screen has a Report button for AI-written text that is offensive or wrong. It opens your email app with the transcript and the cleaned text addressed to the developer. Nothing is sent unless you send that email.
